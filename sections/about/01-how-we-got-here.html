How we got here

We didn't start with an app. We started by listening.

Through the National Science Foundation's I-Corps program, we interviewed faculty and administrators across Northern California. We asked what they were already doing for student wellbeing, and what got in the way.

We heard the same story again and again. Faculty were already checking in on students, often on their own time. One instructor had invented her own start-of-class check-in before we ever described ours. Others ran newsletters, set up peer mentoring, or kept track of students on a whiteboard.

The care was real. What was missing was anything that lasted. Programs ran on one-time grants and stipends, and when the person carrying the work moved on, the work went with them.

That changed our approach. Faculty aren't the problem to solve. They're the people to trust. Campus Mind is built to turn what faculty already do into something their campus can keep.

What we heard matches the research. A review of studies on college instructors found that faculty already talk with and refer struggling students, and want training, clear policies and institutional backing to do it well (source). Research on grant-funded campus programs finds they continue only when institutions commit stable funding, leadership and alignment with campus priorities (source).
